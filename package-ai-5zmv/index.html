01 — The setup

A product built on conversation, from a company the market rarely hears from.

The whole Package.AI argument is that the last mile stops being a notification and becomes a relationship. Jenny answers. Customers book and rebook their own windows. Delivery, service, and sales sit in one thread tied to the order instead of three systems tied to nothing. That is not just a feature set, it is a position: the brands that keep talking are the brands that get remembered.

The company has not yet applied that position to itself. HFA named Package.AI its 2026 Partner of the Year. Your own site claims zero customer turnover across five years. Ziv writes for the trade press and speaks from the association's stage. All of it is real, and almost none of it reaches a buyer on an ordinary Tuesday. The evidence exists in bursts, around events and awards, then goes quiet for weeks at a time.

Quiet is expensive in this particular market, because of where these deals actually stall. Package.AI replaces systems that furniture retailers have run for ten years. The operations lead evaluating you is not lying awake worried the product will underperform. He is worried about being the person who moved the company off something that worked. Price is rarely the objection. Technical fit is rarely the objection.

The objection is unspoken, and it sounds like: can I defend this in the room?

That changes the shape of the work. This is not thought leadership for its own sake and it is not brand awareness for a company that already has a category. Every post, every note from Ziv, every email in this program exists to arm a champion with something forwardable: a named retailer, a real number, an award their boss has heard of, a peer who already made the switch and did not regret it.

It also matters that DispatchTrack is running a comparison page and paid search against your name. Being named by a competitor is a form of recognition, but it means a buyer doing homework can meet someone else's framing of Package.AI before they meet yours. Consistent owned content is the cheapest correction available. When a buyer looks you up, your voice should already be in their feed and in their inbox.

One thing this proposal does not do is change who owns marketing. Rachel has just brought it in-house, which is the right call. Positioning, product truth, and final approval stay at Package.AI. What we add underneath is the production layer: a calendar that fills every week whether or not anyone has time that week, and a lifecycle program that runs on schedule instead of when someone remembers. Presence first. The ABM, webinar, and search programs Ziv and Tamir talked through sit downstream of this, and every one of them lands harder once there is a body of work to point at.

02 — What we heard

Six things that came out of the conversation.

  • /01

    Marketing came in-house. We are the layer underneath it

    Congratulations on the hire. Nothing here replaces it. Rachel sets direction and holds the pen on approvals; Noetic supplies the hours, the production, and the discipline of a calendar that does not slip when a launch week gets busy.

  • /02

    The deal stalls on proof, not on product

    US executives hesitate to move off legacy systems because of what the decision says about them, not because of price or capability. Content that names customers, cites numbers, and shows peers who already switched is the direct answer to that hesitation.

  • /03

    A competitor is already framing you publicly

    DispatchTrack runs a dedicated comparison page and paid search against the Package.AI name. You cannot stop that, and litigating it head-on is usually a losing move. What works is out-publishing it, so a buyer researching the category meets your evidence first and theirs second.

  • /04

    Campaigns yes. Automated outreach no

    A clear line, and we agree with it. Nothing in this scope sends an unsolicited message to anyone. Every channel here is owned: your company page, Ziv's account, and a list of people who opted in or who your team already met.

  • /05

    Ziv is already a category voice, just not a weekly one

    Bylined trade articles and association keynotes are the hard part, and that work is already done. What is missing is frequency. An executive who shows up once a quarter is a speaker. An executive who shows up once a week is a reference point buyers check before they call anyone.

  • /06

    The industry calendar is the spine

    Market weeks and association events are known months ahead, and they are where your buyers concentrate. Planned properly, a three-day booth becomes eight weeks of content: the point of view going in, the capture on the floor, and the follow-through for everyone who stopped by and everyone who did not.

Two voice guides, company and founder

The company page and Ziv's account cannot sound the same, and the fastest way to make executive content read as ghostwritten is to let them. Two separate guides, built from what already exists.

Before writing anything we catalogue what Package.AI can actually claim in public. This is the raw material the whole program runs on, and most companies have more of it than they think.

  • Product moments worth showing rather than describing

Where a claim is not yet cleared for public use, it stays out until it is. We flag it rather than soften it.
