01. What we discussed

A strong product, a one-person marketing team, and a market that does not know it has a choice.

  • /01

    Where Slice is today

    Slice is a global equity management platform: cap table and options, connected to the HRIS so joiners, leavers and relocations flow through automatically, with compliance and tax alerts across jurisdictions and an audit for every new customer. About 200 companies use it. Demo-to-close runs around 30%. Churn is effectively zero. The gap is at the top: not enough of the right companies are reaching the demo.

  • /02

    The ideal customer

    US companies first, since that is the largest market. Series A and beyond, roughly 100+ employees, and ideally with employees in more than one country. Seed-stage companies were ruled out as too early: the contract value is too small and the cap table is still simple. Post-A funding raised is a useful qualifier.

  • /03

    The buying committee

    Three functions touch the decision: Finance, HR and Legal. Finance is the decision maker. HR and Legal influence and often feel the pain first, but if Finance is not convinced, the deal does not move. Paid activity so far has focused on Finance on LinkedIn, with short tests on Meta and Google. The audiences were tested; the message was not yet cracked.

  • /04

    The incumbent problem

    Carta holds an estimated 70-80% of the US market. It became the standard because it replaced spreadsheets, and for most companies "equity management" and "Carta" mean the same thing. People arrive at Slice demos frustrated with their current setup and surprised that an alternative exists. The first job is education: equity management is more than a cap table.

  • /05

    The ecosystem channel

    VCs are the hardest audience: many know Carta and tell portfolio companies not to switch. Slice is building a partnership motion toward VCs, law firms and CPA firms. We agreed this channel should not be approached with classic lead generation. It calls for webinars, professional content and co-marketing that makes the partner the hero for their portfolio.

  • /06

    What Slice is looking for

    One partner that buys the media, writes the content, produces the creative and understands the US buyer, rather than a media buyer here and a creative freelancer there. Slice has a new HubSpot instance with no lifecycle activity yet, and Google, Meta and LinkedIn accounts ready to share. Tracking, attribution and dashboards were flagged as must-haves from day one.

02. The frame

Three functions, three fears, one urgency.

Slice does not need to out-feature Carta. It needs Finance, HR and Legal to feel the cost of never having asked the question. Each function has a different fear. The campaign speaks to each one in its own language, and every message ends in the same place: the companies that look like you are already moving.

The buying committee, as described in the meeting
Track A. Finance

The decision maker

"How much is this costing me that I cannot see?"

Equity is budgeted, forecast and audited every round. Unvested equity from leavers, a contractor in Spain managed in a spreadsheet, a filing missed in a second country: each one shows up later as cost. Finance cares about compliance because Finance pays for it.

MessageCleaner cap table, fewer surprises at the next round, a company that is easier to acquire.
ProofOne compliance alert saved a Slice customer what could have been a $12M exposure.
Track B. Legal

The one who signs off

"What did we file wrong, and when will someone find it?"

Granting equity to an employee in another country means local documents, local filings, local tax withholding. Most companies do not know what they do not know until the audit. Legal's fear is not the rule. It is being the person who missed it.

MessageYou do not want to find out at the audit. Slice flags it before it becomes a finding.
ToneDirect, slightly provocative. This is the one audience where a little fear is honest.
Track C. HR

The one who lives in it

"Did that person actually come off the cap table?"

HR onboards, offboards and relocates people in the HRIS. When the equity system does not listen, a leaver keeps vesting and nobody notices until they try to exercise. HR's fear is a mistake with a person's name on it.

MessageOne system of record. When HR moves someone, equity moves with them.
ToneWarm, practical, human. Fewer mistakes, better employee experience.

Why the whole ecosystem points at one answer, and what to do about it

A founder raises a round. The VC says "just use Carta." The law firm sets up the cap table in Carta because that is what they know. The CPA firm reads from it. Carta itself runs a permanent campaign to stay the default. Nobody in this chain is choosing; they are repeating. That is why Slice cannot win by arguing features. It wins by making the default feel like a risk.

Two moves work together. To the buying committee: urgency and social proof, US names first. The companies that look like yours, in your city, at your stage, have already moved. Nobody wants to be the CFO explaining why they were last. To the ecosystem: make the partner the hero. A VC, law firm or CPA who brings Slice to their portfolio looks forward-thinking and keeps the relationship. That is a webinar and content play, not an ad.

The line between the two is the same: the question everyone stopped asking is the one worth asking again.

SERVICE / 01

Paid Social & ABM

Three messages, one account list, every function covered.

LinkedIn is where Finance, HR and Legal already are, and it is where Slice has already spent the most. The difference is not the platform. It is running one account list with three creative tracks, so the CFO, the GC and the Head of People at the same company each see the message written for them, and then see each other's.

  • Build the target list by stage and size: Series A+, 100+ employees, international headcount. ZoomInfo and LinkedIn native audiences, not an enterprise ABM platform this early.
  • Three creative tracks per campaign (Finance, Legal, HR), same accounts, function-matched messaging.
  • Document ads and gated content over hard demo asks. Warm the account before the sales touch.
  • Social proof built into the creative: US customer names, stages and cities, so the reader recognizes their own company.
  • Custom conversion events, attribution and a live dashboard from week one.

SERVICE / 02

Paid Search

Catch the moment someone finally types "Carta alternative."

Social creates the doubt. Search is where the doubt becomes a query. Slice's own demo calls prove the intent exists: people arrive already frustrated with their current setup. Paid search is how Slice shows up at that exact moment, before the person decides the answer is "just deal with it."

  • Competitor and alternative intent captured with dedicated landing pages, not the homepage.
  • Pain-based queries for each function: international equity compliance, 409A across countries, HRIS to cap table sync, employee equity in a second country.
  • Brand protection for Slice's own name as awareness grows.
  • Landing pages that speak the same function-specific language as the social tracks.
  • A dedicated search specialist. In our experience, a strong paid social buyer is rarely a strong search buyer.

SERVICE / 03

Creative & UGC

The creative engine Slice does not have yet.

Messaging only works if the creative carries it. Slice needs a steady flow of function-specific ads, short video, gated content and interactive tools, all in one visual language, all in US business English. That is an in-house engine, not a freelancer. It is also where social proof becomes visible: real customers, on camera, in their own words.

  • A creative system built from Slice's existing brand guidelines, then executed across every format.
  • Customer-led UGC: short, honest videos from Finance, HR and Legal leaders already on Slice. The most convincing ad is a peer.
  • Interactive gated tools, like an equity compliance check or a "what does your cap table cost you" calculator, that warm a lead before anyone asks for a demo.
  • Ongoing testing: message, format and hook, measured against qualified pipeline, not clicks.
  • Landing pages, emails and ads written by the same team so the story stays consistent.

SERVICE / 04

Lifecycle & Email

HubSpot is set up. Now it needs a job.

Slice's HubSpot is a month old and empty. That is a clean start. Every lead from social, search and the ecosystem channel needs a path: a nurture built for its function, triggered by what it did, ending in a demo request or a sales handoff. This is also the engine behind the VC, law firm and CPA webinar program.

  • HubSpot foundation: lists, lifecycle stages, lead scoring, source attribution, sales handoff rules.
  • Three nurture tracks matched to the three message tracks. A Legal lead gets Legal emails.
  • Behavior-based follow-up: downloaded, watched, visited pricing, went quiet. Each gets a different next step.
  • Webinar operations for the ecosystem channel: platform, promotion, registration, reminders, on-demand and post-event follow-up.
  • Dashboards that tie every lead back to the ad, page and email that created it.
